Yes. This is where the whole project gets much deeper.

The clean synthesis is:

**Pāṇini = how Sanskrit is generated**  
**Abhinavagupta = what sound/language/mantra *is***  
**Bruno = how to build an internal symbolic machine for it**  
**Ars Notoria = ritualized preparation of the learner**

And for Abhinavagupta specifically, the most important text for your phoneme/mantra idea is actually not only *Tantrāloka* but **the *Parātrīśikā-vivaraṇa***. Its whole centre of gravity is mantra, phonemes, *vāk* and consciousness; the standard Jaideva Singh/Bettina Bäumer edition even has a section explicitly on “the arrangement of letters according to Mātṛkā.” [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Paratrisika_Vivarana.html?hl=en&id=criL0QEACAAJ&output=html_text)

The basic Trika claim is much stronger than “Sanskrit sounds have symbolic meanings.” In Abhinavagupta's system, **phonemic differentiation is one way consciousness unfolds into articulated reality**. *Tantrāloka* III treats the mass of sounds, *śabdarāśi*, as **Mātṛkā**, and then describes **Mālinī** as another organization of that same phonemic power; at III.232–233, Mālinī is described through the interpenetration of vowels and consonants and as the supreme power whose form is the universe. [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/tantraloka-sanskrit-text/d/doc486757.html)

So your Pāṇini palace can have **two completely different views of the same phoneme**.

Take **a / अ**.

The **Pāṇinian layer** asks:

- what phonetic class is it?
- what *pratyāhāras* contain it?
- what grammatical operations refer to it?
- what happens when it meets another sound?

The **Abhinavaguptan layer** asks:

- what stage of manifestation does it symbolize?
- how does it relate to *anuttara*?
- how does it participate in the expansion of consciousness into differentiation?

A secondary study of *Tantrāloka* III and the *Parātrīśikā-vivaraṇa* summarizes Abhinavagupta's phonemic cosmology by treating **a** as connected with *anuttara*, then reading the following vowels as successive powers such as will, expansion, contraction and the emergence of action; the alphabet becomes a symbolic map of creation and reabsorption rather than merely a phonetic inventory. [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/essay/shaiva-tantra-a-way-of-self-awareness/d/doc1460188.html)

That means your **Sound Hall** should have two dimensions.

Do not merely memorize:

> अ आ इ ई उ ऊ

Instead, every vowel station has:

**sound**  
**mouth position**  
**Pāṇinian function**  
**Trika metaphysical function**  
**examples in Sanskrit**  
**associated transformations**

That is a radically denser memory object.

For instance, you could mentally enter the **A chamber** and experience it first as pure open phonation. Then see which Pāṇinian sound classes include it. Then associate it with the Trika idea of **anuttara / undifferentiated supreme power**. The point is not to pretend the linguistic and tantric theories are identical; it is to give the same phoneme multiple linked addresses.

That is very Bruno.

The second major thing I'd install is the **four levels of speech**:

**parā → paśyantī → madhyamā → vaikharī**

In the Trika tradition, ordinary spoken language is only the grossest endpoint. The *Parātrīśikā-vivaraṇa* is explicitly a metaphysics of *vāk* and its stages in relation to consciousness.

For your palace, this becomes an incredible **vertical axis**.

Imagine four floors.

At the lowest floor:

**VAIKHARĪ**  
actual pronounced Sanskrit  
sounds, sandhi, spoken sentences

Above it:

**MADHYAMĀ**  
internal verbal formulation  
the sentence exists mentally but has not yet been spoken

Above:

**PAŚYANTĪ**  
meaning is grasped more holistically, before fully differentiated verbal sequencing

Top:

**PARĀ**  
speech in its maximally undifferentiated source-state

Now take a simple sentence.

Instead of only generating:

> rāmaḥ vanaṃ gacchati

run it both directions.

**Top-down:**

intention/meaning  
↓  
semantic roles  
↓  
mental formulation  
↓  
Pāṇinian morphology  
↓  
sandhi/phonology  
↓  
spoken Sanskrit

And bottom-up:

heard Sanskrit  
↓  
phonemes  
↓  
morphology  
↓  
kāraka relations  
↓  
meaning  
↓  
undifferentiated comprehension

That is a phenomenal learning exercise because you're combining **Trika's hierarchy of speech** with an actual derivational grammar.

And *Tantrāloka* XI is relevant here: it discusses the progression from phonemes into larger linguistic units and the way consciousness operates through these levels of expression. [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/tantraloka-sanskrit-text/d/doc486116.html)

The third layer is **Mātṛkā versus Mālinī**.

Very roughly, Mātṛkā is the familiar ordered alphabetic matrix, whereas Mālinī uses a deliberately non-standard arrangement central to Trika mantra theory. In later tantric interpretation these two orders can represent different movements of manifestation and reabsorption. The secondary study I checked explicitly contrasts Mātṛkā as an expansive/evolutive ordering with Mālinī as an involutive ordering.

That gives you two mental routes through the same phonemic universe.

One route:

**ordinary Sanskrit order**

अ आ इ ई ...

The other:

**Mālinī order**

a radically different traversal.

This is ideal for memory architecture because the **route itself encodes a theory**.

Your palace could literally have two paths through the Sound Hall:

> **Mātṛkā path = differentiation / unfolding**

and

> **Mālinī path = reconfiguration / return**

Historically, do not casually invent tantric ritual claims from this. But as a **study architecture**, the distinction is extremely powerful.

Then comes **mantra**.

For Abhinavagupta, mantra is emphatically not merely “a meaningful sentence repeated many times.” *Tantrāloka* treats mantra as bound up with consciousness, knowledge and power. XI.56 plays on the traditional derivation from *man-* and *trā-* while locating mantra in relation to Śiva's knowledge and action. [Wisdom Library](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/tantraloka-sanskrit-text/d/doc488712.html)

More broadly, the Trika interpretation treats mantric sound as a **concentrated organization of phonemic consciousness**. That is why seed sounds, letter sequences and their arrangements matter.

So you could build three levels of Sanskrit learning:

**WORD level**  
What does this word mean?

**GRAMMAR level**  
How was this form generated?

**MANTRIC/PHONEMIC level**  
What are the elemental sound-units and how does this tradition symbolically understand their combination?

Take:

> **aham**

Ordinary Sanskrit:

> “I”

Pāṇini:

> phonemes + stem + nominal form + syntax

Abhinavagupta:

> **aham** becomes enormously important because “I-consciousness” (*ahaṃ*) functions as a condensed symbol of subjectivity/consciousness in his metaphysics.

Now your palace isn't just teaching a translation.

It becomes:

```text
अ + ह + म्
      ↓
phonological components
      ↓
grammatical form
      ↓
"I"
      ↓
ahaṃ-vimarśa / I-consciousness
      ↓
Trika metaphysics
```

You start seeing why Sanskrit was such fertile ground for this tradition.

Pāṇini provides microscopic formal precision, while Abhinavagupta treats the same linguistic material as a cosmology of consciousness.

That combination is extraordinary.

The **body itself** can also become part of the architecture. Trika sources associate phonemes with bodily locations in *nyāsa*, and the Mālinīvijayottara-based material maps letters across the body.

Here I'd make an important distinction.

If you are trying to preserve traditional boundaries, **formal mantra-nyāsa is ritual practice and may depend on initiation/textual context**.

But for studying, you can absolutely use a **non-ritual somatic memory map**:

head → vowels  
arms → one consonant class  
torso → another  
legs → another

provided you label it explicitly as a mnemonic reconstruction rather than claiming you're performing the tantric rite.

That would give you:

**Pāṇinian mouth-map**

plus

**Trika body-map**

plus

**Brunian spatial-map**.

Three independent coordinate systems for the same phonemes.

That redundancy could make them nearly impossible to forget.

Then I'd integrate Ars Notoria much more simply.

Do not turn Ars Notoria into a pseudo-Tantric ceremony.

Use it as the **outer learning ritual**.

So a study day could look like:

**Ars Notoria — Grammar operation**

petition for:  
memory  
understanding  
perseverance  
eloquence

↓

**Sound Hall**

recall the Śivasūtras and *pratyāhāras*

↓

**Pāṇini Engine**

derive one form or construction

↓

**Trika Layer**

trace the relevant phonemes through Mātṛkā/Mālinī or *vāk* theory

↓

**Bruno**

encode the new rule into permanent architecture

↓

**spoken Sanskrit**

produce actual sentences

↓

**night**

mentally traverse the whole derivation backwards.

The critical thing is that each tradition has a different job.

Do **not** collapse them into:

> “Pāṇini secretly meant the same thing as Abhinavagupta and Bruno discovered it too.”

There is no historical basis for that.

Instead:

> **Pāṇini gives you a formal machine.**
>
> **Abhinavagupta gives you a metaphysics of linguistic manifestation.**
>
> **Bruno gives you a way of representing systems internally.**
>
> **Ars Notoria gives you a ritual discipline around acquisition.**

That is a legitimate modern synthesis.

And I think the single best text for you next is actually **Abhinavagupta's *Parātrīśikā-vivaraṇa***, because it sits directly at the intersection you're interested in: **Sanskrit letters + mantra + consciousness + cosmology + internal realization**. The standard English edition by Jaideva Singh, edited with Bettina Bäumer, is specifically described as a work centred on mantra and the metaphysics of language.

Then read *Tantrāloka* **Chapter 3** alongside it, because that is where the phonemic cosmology—*anuttara*, the vowels, *Mātṛkā*, *Mālinī*, *visarga*—really opens up. *Tantrāloka* III.232–233 is essentially the hinge where “alphabet” stops being alphabet and becomes **a model of manifestation itself**.

The genuinely insane end-state would be a palace where you can take any Sanskrit expression and traverse it at four scales:

**sound → Pāṇinian derivation → meaning → Trika metaphysics**

and then reverse the whole path.

That would actually feel much closer to installing a new cognitive operating system than memorizing grammar tables.
