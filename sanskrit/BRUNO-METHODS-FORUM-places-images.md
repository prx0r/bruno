runo isn’t complex, but his books are a mixture of other methods known to medieval scholars, so it’s not that he’s difficult to explain, but explaining him to someone who doesn’t understand him would be a lot of work, which is why you don’t have many answers.

But if you look at it from the perspective of memory palaces—that is, places and images—that’s all there is to it.

He creates lists of images like places, also using them to memorize words like some alphabetic systems and also as indexes for information. His palaces are interconnected, and he creates knowledge trees in which he meditates using Ramon Llull’s system (Llull’s system uses Aristotelian categories, which are ways of asking questions) to delve deeper into a certain topic. But by using so many ways of doing things, it becomes difficult to explain without requiring pages. He mixes Ravenna’s methods, alphabetic methods that would be basically like a Pao but for memorizing words. His places are square and he turns them into practically a museum with images by categories, using the alphabet for greater order, and thus he goes from room A, B, C… where each one is ordered by angles and those angles contain images. I prefer to use the table, which is a 3x3 grid on the walls, and use the 6 areas of a room, which gives me 54 loci in a room. In each square, you place a short story that could serve as a guide. For example, you could use Spiderman as the room for “S” and include 54 associated items, such as Spiderman or Peter Parker in square 1, Venom in square 2, Sandman in square 3, Spiderman stopping the train in square 4, and so on, up to 54. It’s best if the topics are familiar, as this will make the process faster.

Well, the grid shown is one of his methods, but as you can see, explaining each one takes a lot of time. Just keep in mind that these are methods for memorizing and delving deeper into topics of interest. Don’t get obsessed with the methods if you don’t understand them; just extract what you need to improve.

For example, in a certain part of De Umbris where he talks about places, the atoms are loci like a chair, window, etc., but this would be strange nowadays, but in his time when he wrote it, it was understood what he meant; you go from a house, which is a general place, to a chair, which is specific.
