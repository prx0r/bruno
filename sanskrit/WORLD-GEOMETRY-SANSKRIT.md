Yes. The edition I’d get is:

**Abhinavagupta, _Parātrīśikā-Vivaraṇa: The Secret of Tantric Mysticism_**, translated by **Jaideva Singh**, edited by **Bettina Bäumer**, with the Sanskrit text corrected and technical notes/charts supplied by **Swami Lakshman Joo**, Motilal Banarsidass.

It is Sanskrit + English, with running exposition rather than a bare translation. The publisher describes it as centred on **anuttara, mantra, the metaphysics of language, vāk, and the stages of speech**, and the contents explicitly include a substantial section on the **arrangement of letters according to Mātṛkā**. [Motilal Banarsidass](https://www.motilalbanarsidass.com/products/para-trisika-vivarana-of-abhinavagupta-the-secret-of-tantric-mysticism)

[Official Motilal Banarsidass edition](https://www.motilalbanarsidass.com/products/para-trisika-vivarana-of-abhinavagupta-the-secret-of-tantric-mysticism)

For what you're doing, this is probably **better than beginning with the entire _Tantrāloka_**. It goes straight into the material you care about: phonemes, mantra, *aham*, Mātṛkā/Mālinī, *vāk*, manifestation and consciousness.

One caveat: it's an interpretive traditional translation, heavily informed by Lakshman Joo's living Kashmir Śaiva lineage. That is a major strength for understanding the system from inside the tradition, but for eventual academic work I would keep the Sanskrit beside it and later compare modern philological scholarship. The edition itself contains both Sanskrit and English, so you can grow into exactly that.

And yes: **“world-building where Sanskrit phonemes and grammar become the geometry of the world”** is basically the most interesting formulation of your project so far.

Not because Pāṇini secretly taught Trika metaphysics. He didn't. But you can give **different dimensions of the same Sanskrit material different kinds of geometry**.

Imagine the world having four simultaneous layers.

### 1. The phonetic geometry

The very substrate of the world is Sanskrit sound.

Not alphabet cards floating around. Actual **articulation-space**:

**kaṇṭha** — throat  
**tālu** — palate  
**mūrdhan** — retroflex region  
**danta** — teeth  
**oṣṭha** — lips

So moving through the world corresponds to moving through the vocal tract.

क ख ग घ ङ belong together spatially because they are structurally related sounds.

Then Pāṇini's Śivasūtras overlay another coordinate system:

> अ इ उ ण्  
> ऋ ऌ क्  
> ए ओ ङ्  
> ...

Those aren't merely things to memorize. They create **addressable sets of phonemes** through *pratyāhāra*.

So the first layer of reality is already a compressed symbolic geometry.

### 2. Pāṇini becomes the physics engine

Then the world gets laws.

A form entering one region encounters a rule.

A sound crosses a boundary.

It changes.

Two words collide.

Sandhi happens.

A verbal root enters a derivation machine.

Affixes appear.

Markers disappear.

Substitutions fire.

Competing rules resolve.

So instead of:

> memorize `iko yaṇ aci`

you construct an actual **physical law of the world**.

Whenever an appropriate sound encounters the appropriate condition, the transformation occurs automatically.

Eventually you wouldn't “remember the rule.”

You would intuit:

> **that's what happens here.**

That's exactly what an internalized grammar ultimately is.

### 3. Bruno supplies symbolic topology

Bruno lets you make all of this **navigable**.

Different relationships deserve different geometry:

**sequence → road**

**classification → tree**

**transformation → machine**

**combination → wheel**

**semantic role → person/statue**

**rule hierarchy → tower**

**phonological boundary → gate**

**exception → hidden passage**

**meta-rule → control room**

This is much more powerful than forcing everything into rooms.

You're building a **heterogeneous cognitive world** in which spatial form expresses logical form.

That's very close to the deeper Renaissance memory-art ambition: internal order rather than mere recall.

### 4. Abhinavagupta gives the world metaphysical depth

And now it gets strange.

From the Trika perspective, articulated language is not just human labels pasted onto an already finished universe.

The movement from relatively undifferentiated consciousness toward differentiated speech parallels the emergence of differentiated manifestation.

So your world gets a **vertical axis**:

```text
PARĀ
undifferentiated speech-consciousness

        ↓

PAŚYANTĪ
meaning appearing as an integrated whole

        ↓

MADHYAMĀ
internally differentiated language

        ↓

VAIKHARĪ
actual articulated phonemes
```

Then your Pāṇinian city exists largely toward the **vaikharī / differentiated** end.

But you can climb upward.

Take a Sanskrit utterance.

At street level:

> sounds.

Walk upward:

> morphology.

Higher:

> semantic relationships.

Higher:

> unified intention/meaning.

Higher still:

> consciousness before the subject/object structure has completely differentiated.

Now learning Sanskrit becomes simultaneously:

**pronunciation → grammar → semantics → philosophy of cognition.**

And you can traverse it **both directions**.

That is what makes your “reconstructing reality” language appropriate **within Abhinavagupta's metaphysics**. You're not claiming you've discovered how physical reality scientifically works. You're deliberately constructing an internal world that mirrors **his account of manifestation through consciousness, differentiation and language**.

## And Mātṛkā makes this even better

This is exactly why the Singh/Bäumer volume matters.

Its table of contents contains **“The arrangement of letters according to Mātṛkā.”**

You can eventually have two great phonemic structures:

**Pāṇini's phonemes**

organized according to **grammatical efficiency**.

And:

**Abhinavagupta's phonemes**

organized according to **mantric/metaphysical significance**.

Same Sanskrit sounds.

Completely different topology.

That's intellectually beautiful because you don't need to claim one secretly derives from the other.

You can switch views:

> **PĀṆINI MODE:** what operations does this sound participate in?

> **TRIKA MODE:** what does this phonemic differentiation signify within this metaphysics of consciousness?

> **BRUNO MODE:** where/how should I represent those relationships so that I can retrieve and manipulate them?

And above all of them:

> **ARS NOTORIA MODE:** cultivate memory, understanding, perseverance and eloquence so I can actually master the system.

That gives you a genuinely coherent four-layer project.

### I would read in this order

Start the **Singh/Bäumer _Parātrīśikā-Vivaraṇa_** now, but don't attempt to digest everything.

Read initially for four concepts:

**anuttara → aham → Mātṛkā/Mālinī → vāk/mantra.**

Meanwhile learn:

**Sanskrit sounds → Śivasūtras → basic pratyāhāras.**

Your first mental structure can therefore be tiny:

```text
                ANUTTARA
                   │
                   │
                  AHAM
                   │
            phonemic emergence
                   │
       ┌───────────┴───────────┐
     MĀTṚKĀ                  MĀLINĪ
       │
    PHONEMES
       │
   PĀṆINIAN SOUND SYSTEM
       │
 grammatical transformation
       │
       WORD
       │
     SENTENCE
       │
     SPEECH
```

Then gradually **turn that diagram into somewhere you can actually enter**.

And I wouldn't rush that process. You're potentially building something you could use for years: Sanskrit grammar, *Tantrāloka*, mantra theory, philosophy, eventually whole texts could all become inhabitants of the same internal world rather than separate things sitting in books.

That's the point at which “memory palace” undersells what you're attempting.

It's closer to constructing an **internal symbolic environment in which you can think Sanskrit philosophy from inside its own categories.**
